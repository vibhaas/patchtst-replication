PatchTST replication · Architecture

How PatchTST makes a forecast

The supervised model path used in our experiments

Past valuesA window of readings from several variables
→
NormalizeRevIN adjusts each channel using its own window
→
Make patchesOverlapping groups of nearby values become tokens
→
Embed patchesProject each patch and add its position
→
Shared TransformerAttention compares patches within each channel
→
Predict futureA shared head forecasts; inverse RevIN restores the scale
Channel independence: each variable follows this path separately. The encoder and forecast head reuse the same learned weights for every variable.

Our patches contain 16 values and move forward by 8 values each time. The forecast head maps the encoded patches to the requested future horizon.

PatchTST replication · Conclusion

What we conclude

Our reading of the results, the ablation, and the training curves

We think we reproduced the paper's main pattern, though this was not a complete reproduction of every experiment.
  • Our results on the smaller datasets and Weather stayed close to the published results. This suggests our model and evaluation followed the intended method.
  • Removing patching or channel independence made forecasts worse in our ablation. That supports the paper's explanation for why PatchTST works.
  • The training curves show that the models learned and then improved more slowly. They do not suggest that the runs simply failed to train.
  • The larger datasets were harder on our laptop. We shortened training and changed parts of the setup, so we cannot treat every remaining gap as a problem with the model itself.
  • Overall, we would call this a successful check of the central idea. Matching every published result would need longer, more controlled runs.
